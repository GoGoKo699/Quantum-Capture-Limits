# Zero versus positive quantum capacity

The receiver-specific boundary follows by applying established damping–dephasing and channel-extension results to the full temporal input space. The [simulation theorem](channel-simulation.md) identifies the best receiver schedule; the boundary here decides whether its capacity is zero. It does not determine the positive capacity value.

## Channel for a fixed temporal mode

The one-photon Fock-state equations give [B12](references.md#b12),

```math
\mathcal E_f(\rho)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_{00}+(1-p)\rho_{11}&m^*\rho_{01}\\
m\rho_{10}&p\rho_{11}
\end{pmatrix},
```

with

```math
p=\beta\langle f,K_rf\rangle,\qquad
m=\sqrt\beta\langle h_r,f\rangle,
```

```math
K_r(u,v)=e^{-(u+v)/2-r|u-v|},\qquad
h_r(u)=e^{-(r+1/2)u},\qquad\|f\|_2=1.
```

Complete positivity follows from $K_r-|h_r\rangle\langle h_r|\succeq0$; [Pulse objectives](pulse-objectives.md) supplies its Gram representation. Also $K_r\succeq0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}K_r=1$. Therefore $0\le |m|^2\le p\le\beta$. A known phase can make $m\ge0$ without changing any result below.

This is a damping–dephasing channel [SAJS24](references.md#sajs24), with damping probability $g=1-p$ and phase-flip probability $d=(1-m/\sqrt p)/2$ when $p>0$. The condition below is not a new classification of this channel family.

### Exact zero/positive test

```math
\boxed{Q(\mathcal E_f)>0\iff p+m^2>1.}
```

**Zero side.** For $p+m^2\le1$, define a two-output channel with Kraus operators (output order 00,01,10,11)

```math
F_0=|00\rangle\langle0|
+m(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)\langle1|,
```
```math
F_1=\sqrt{p-m^2}|11\rangle\langle1|,
\qquad
F_2=\sqrt{1-p-m^2}|00\rangle\langle1|.
```

They satisfy $\sum_jF_j^\dagger F_j=I$. Tracing out either output gives exactly $\mathcal E_f$. Such an extension is equivalent to antidegradability: the environment can simulate the receiver. A positive unassisted transmission rate would then permit asymptotically faithful decoding by two receivers, contradicting no-cloning. The extension broadcasts the *noisy channel output*, not two perfect copies of the input state.

As an independent check, the normalized Choi state has zero determinant, and

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(J_B^2)-\operatorname{Tr}(J_{AB}^2)
=\frac{1-p-m^2}{2}.
```

The known two-qubit symmetric-extension criterion [CJ14](references.md#cj14) gives the same antidegradability region.

**Positive side.** For $\rho_q=\operatorname{diag}(1-q,q)$, write $s=m^2$. The environment has eigenvalues

```math
e_0=(1-p)q,\qquad
 e_\pm=\frac{t\pm\sqrt{t^2-4(p-s)q(1-q)}}2,
\quad t=1-(1-p)q.
```

The receiver eigenvalues are $pq$ and $1-pq$. The coherent information obeys

```math
I_c(q)=[p+s-1]q\log_2(1/q)+O(q).
```

It is positive for sufficiently small nonzero $q$ when $p+s>1$. Positive coherent information is an achievable unassisted quantum-communication rate. This is the established logarithmic-entropy method, explicitly applied to damping–dephasing channels in [SAJS24, Lemma 1](references.md#sajs24).

For checking very small numbers, retain the linear coefficient:

```math
I_c(q)=\frac q{\ln2}
\left[D\ln(1/q)+D-p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)+(p-s)\ln(p-s)\right]
+O(q^2\ln(1/q)),
\quad D=p+s-1.
```

No optimization over all input states or multiple copies is needed to prove positivity. Conversely, the expression does not give the exact capacity above the threshold. Damping–dephasing coherent information can be superadditive [SAJS24](references.md#sajs24) and [BL25](references.md#bl25).

## A stronger statement: arbitrary temporal encoding in the one-photon sector

Let $\mathcal H_1=L^2([0,\infty))$ be the one-photon temporal space. A general input on $\mathbb C|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\oplus\mathcal H_1$ has block form

```math
\rho=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}&\rho_{01}\\\rho_{10}&\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}.
```

Define $A=\beta K_r$ and $|b\rangle=\sqrt\beta|h_r\rangle$. By polarization of the one-photon response, the receiver map is

```math
\mathcal E(\rho)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_{00}+\operatorname{Tr}[(I-A)\rho_{11}]&\rho_{01}|b\rangle\\
\langle b|\rho_{10}&\operatorname{Tr}(A\rho_{11})
\end{pmatrix}.
```

The operator inequalities $0\preceq|b\rangle\langle b|\preceq A\preceq I$ guarantee complete positivity. More generally, this form describes a vacuum-preserving qubit-output channel with no coherence generated by fixed-one-photon inputs.

If

```math
A+|b\rangle\langle b|\preceq I,
```

there is an explicit all-mode broadcast extension. Choose an orthonormal basis $\{|j\rangle\}$ of $\mathcal H_1$ and set

```math
F_0=|00\rangle\langle\mathrm{vac}|
+(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)\langle b|,
```
```math
F_{1j}=|11\rangle\langle j|\sqrt{A-|b\rangle\langle b|},
\quad
F_{2j}=|00\rangle\langle j|\sqrt{I-A-|b\rangle\langle b|}.
```

The countable Kraus sum is trace preserving, and both receiver marginals equal E. Hence the entire input channel is antidegradable, including every temporal encoding and every entangled code over independent uses.

If the inequality fails, there is a normalized f for which

```math
\langle f,(A+|b\rangle\langle b|)f\rangle>1.
```

The subspace $\operatorname{span}\{|\mathrm{vac}\rangle,|1_f\rangle\}$ then has positive capacity by the fixed-mode criterion above. Thus the general operator inequality is both necessary and sufficient for the zero-capacity side in this channel class. This conclusion does not rely on a finite discretization or on assuming that a particular pulse is optimal.

The proof is a short composition of known channel arguments, supplied here to avoid overstating a pulse-family-only restriction as an arbitrary-mode theorem. No separate priority claim for the abstract channel-class observation is made.

## Exact receiver spectrum and boundary pulse

The boundary objective is

```math
p+m^2=\beta\langle f,L_rf\rangle,\quad
L_r=K_r+|h_r\rangle\langle h_r|,
```

with kernel

```math
L_r(u,v)=e^{-(u+v)/2}
\left[e^{-r|u-v|}+e^{-r(u+v)}\right].
```

The second exponential changes the boundary condition of the integral equation. If L_rf=Lambda f and

```math
g(u)=\int_0^\infty
[e^{-r|u-v|}+e^{-r(u+v)}]e^{-v/2}f(v)dv,
```

then

```math
g''-r^2g=-\frac{2r}{\Lambda}e^{-u}g,
\qquad g'(0)=0.
```

Setting x=sqrt(8r/Lambda)e^{-u/2} gives Bessel's equation of order nu=2r. Decay at u=infinity selects J_nu. The Neumann condition gives J_nu'(x_0)=0, and the leading eigenvalue is

```math
\Lambda(r)=\frac{8r}{[j'_{2r,1}]^2}.
```

The first eigenfunction is strictly positive. A normalized boundary-optimal pulse is

```math
f_Q(u)=
\frac{e^{-u/2}J_{2r}(x_0e^{-u/2})}
{\sqrt{J_{2r}(x_0)^2-J_{2r-1}(x_0)J_{2r+1}(x_0)}}.
```

At r=0, L_0=2|h_0><h_0|, f_Q=e^{-u/2}, and Lambda=2. The first derivative zero for positive order tends to zero as $r$ approaches zero; substituting the first strictly positive zero of $J_0'$ would give the wrong limit.

The Bessel recurrence at the derivative root yields useful scalar metrics:

```math
m_Q^2=\frac{\beta\Lambda^2}{4-2r\Lambda},
\qquad p_Q=\beta\Lambda-m_Q^2.
```

This pulse maximizes the *margin* p+m²-1 and determines whether any encoding succeeds. It is not claimed to maximize Q itself. In particular, it differs from the average-fidelity-optimal pulse of the pulse-objective analysis.

The largest eigenvalue decreases strictly with r. Although pointwise monotonicity of kernels does not in general imply operator order, here it suffices: the positive maximizing eigenfunction at the larger r has strictly larger Rayleigh quotient in the smaller-r kernel. Also Lambda tends to zero at strong dephasing; a Schur bound on K_r plus ||h_r||² gives this limit. Consequently every beta>1/2 has a unique threshold; beta<=1/2 has no positive-capacity region even in the noise-free limit.

| Incident fraction beta | Largest r allowing positive capacity (strictly below the endpoint) |
|---:|---:|
| 0.50 | None |
| 0.55 | 0.06761278 |
| 0.60 | 0.13696566 |
| 0.75 | 0.35361559 |
| 0.90 | 0.58033427 |
| 1.00 | 0.73580080 |

These are parameter thresholds of the stated model, not experimentally validated universal interface specifications.

## A pulse can absorb less yet change zero capacity to positive capacity

At r=0.72 and beta=1:

| Temporal pulse | Population p | Coherence m | p+m² | Unassisted Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Population optimum | 0.61668899 | 0.61668899 | 0.99699430 | Exactly zero |
| Ordinary exponential f(u)=sqrt(2)e^-u | 0.60060060 | 0.63703314 | 1.00641182 | Strictly positive |
| Exact boundary-margin optimum | 0.60926569 | 0.63317689 | 1.01017866 | Strictly positive |

For the elementary exponential, the criterion is exact rational arithmetic:

```math
p=\frac{200}{333},\qquad m^2=\frac{5000}{12321},\qquad
p+m^2-1=\frac{79}{12321}>0.
```

The population-optimal pulse is certified globally by the [population kernel spectrum](pulse-objectives.md), not just within exponentials. Reducing its population by changing the temporal mode can make coherent communication possible. The numerical separation is robust at the level of the sign criterion; no delicate cancellation in an entropy calculation is needed to establish it.

At beta=1, the thresholds are:

| Pulse selection rule | Positive-capacity threshold r |
|---|---:|
| Maximize population | 0.7154844683 |
| Best boundary objective among rising exponentials | 0.7313754032 |
| Maximize average fidelity over all shapes | 0.7344094125 |
| Maximize the exact capacity-boundary margin | 0.7358007967 |

For an exponential f_a=sqrt(a)e^{-au/2}, the objective is

```math
p+m^2=\frac{4\beta a(2a+c+1)}{(a+1)(a+c)^2},\qquad c=1+2r.
```

Its stationary polynomial is 2a³+2a²+(1-3c)a-c²-c=0. For c>=1 there is exactly one positive root, and the objective vanishes at both endpoints. This proves the exponential-family global optimum. The extension from that family to the exact boundary is only about 0.6% in $r$. The channel-ordering theorem, rather than a large advantage from complicated pulse shapes, is the central result.

## Capacity positivity is not a useful-rate guarantee

The small-input proof can certify remarkably small rates near the boundary. We evaluated the full entropy formulas, not only their asymptotic expansion, at both 90 and 140 decimal digits.

For the elementary r=0.72 exponential and rho_q=diag(1-q,q), q=10^-26 gives

```math
I_c=1.6246241910\ldots\times10^{-28}
\quad\text{qubits per independent use}.
```

For the exact boundary-margin pulse at the same r, the explicit choice

```math
q=1.8633516299\ldots\times10^{-17}
```

gives

```math
I_c=2.7362773681\ldots\times10^{-19}
\quad\text{qubits per independent use}.
```

These are achievable lower bounds. They are **not upper bounds** on the capacity and do not exclude more effective inputs or multi-use codes. We have not solved that optimization. They nevertheless prevent a practical-rate claim from being supported merely by the sign switch. A standard double-precision entropy scan can miss such positivity altogether; the analytic criterion and high-precision witnesses distinguish a true zero from a positive number below ordinary numerical resolution. Entropic singularities and very small positive transmission witnesses are established phenomena [S21, SAJS24].

There is also an independent [upper bound on the full capacity](capacity-upper-bound.md). With $\delta=\beta\Lambda_r(S)-1>0$, it gives $Q=O(\delta\log(1/\delta))$ as the boundary is approached. Thus all codes have vanishing rate there, although the upper bound and these achievable witnesses do not determine the exact rate or its scaling.

The $r=3$ [fidelity example](pulse-objectives.md) illustrates another distinction. Its fidelity-optimal pulse has average fidelity 0.670674>2/3, but the full receiver has

```math
\Lambda(3)=0.4265226440\ldots<1.
```

Thus every allowed temporal encoding has zero unassisted capacity there, despite the fidelity benchmark being exceeded. This is not a contradiction: single-shot uniform-qubit fidelity and asymptotic unassisted communication are different tasks. Nor does zero capacity here imply an entanglement-breaking channel. The single-rail Choi state remains non-positive under partial transpose when m is nonzero; assisted entanglement protocols are a different resource setting.
